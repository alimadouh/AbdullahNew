# Audit: batch A (paracetamol, co-codamol, codeine, tramadol, ibuprofen, naproxen)

Independent second check, 2026-10-17. Every source in `sources/A.json` was re-read in full (SmPC 4.1 to 4.9) from the shared cache. Section 4.4 and 4.5 of every SmPC in batches B to G were searched for these medicines and their groups (NSAID, opioid, analgesic, paracetamol, codeine, tramadol). After the edits, `verify.mjs` reports 4467/4467 quotes found, 0 schema errors and 0 warnings.

Level policy applied in this audit: the brief says "contraindicated" means stated as a contraindication (section 4.3, or "must not"). So anything listed in SmPC section 4.3 is coded `contraindicated`, even when the verb is "should not be used/given" or "use should be avoided". The original policy coded those 4.3 sentences as `avoid`. The notes in `sources/A.json` now record the revised policy.

## 2. Flagged items reviewed (no change, or a change already listed above)

- **Codeine "liver disease" in 4.3 → hepatic `any` contraindicated.** Kept. Both codeine SmPCs list "liver disease" in 4.3 with no severity qualifier. The 4.4 sentence "should be avoided or the dose reduced in patients with renal or hepatic impairment" itself points back to "(see 4.3 Contraindications, liver disease)". So the stronger 4.3 level is the faithful reading. The hepatic item shows both statements. Clinically this is stricter than common practice, so the integrator should know it is literal SmPC text.
- **Naproxen pregnancy.** Kept: T3 contraindicated, T1-T2 avoid "unless clearly necessary", and the conflicting Naprosyn 4.6 sentence shown as an item and in the T1-T2 rule's action. Naprosyn 4.6 ends "Consequently, Naproxen is contraindicated during pregnancy. See point 4.3", but 4.3 of the same SmPC and of Naprosyn EC say last trimester only. The sentence follows the third-trimester paragraph. It reads as an editing error, but it is real UK SmPC text, so it stays visible.
- **Ibuprofen tablet "should not be used" vs suspension contraindicated.** Resolved by #1 to #3.
- **Co-codamol 30/500 extra contraindications scoped to 30/500.** Scope is correct to the sources: the Zentiva 8/500 SmPC (same MAH) does not list acute asthma, respiratory depression, acute alcoholism, head injury, raised ICP, biliary surgery or MAOIs. I added the missing biliary-surgery item to the 30/500 set (#8). Residual concern: a patient with, for example, acute asthma prescribed 8/500 gets no contraindication. The codeine 30 mg rules do not carry across to co-codamol in the engine.
- **Paracetamol maximum dose / body weight.** Resolved by #23. No UK oral paracetamol SmPC used states a weight-based maximum for adults. BNF was not accessed, and NICE CKS is geo-blocked, so a low-body-weight limit remains unsourced in this KB.

## 3. Problems found but not fixed

- **MAOI contraindication or avoidance** (co-codamol 30/500 4.3, tramadol 4.3, codeine 4.4/4.5): no MAOI is in the master list, so no rule is possible. These remain as section text only.
- **Codeine "should not be given to comatose patients"** (4.3): no vocab condition. It is shown in the contraindications item only.
- **Excipient intolerance:**
  - "Should not take": hereditary fructose intolerance (paracetamol and ibuprofen suspensions); galactose intolerance / lactase deficiency (Brufen, codeine, tramadol prolonged-release).
  - None of these are encoded as rules; there are no vocab conditions for them.
  - This is consistent with the rest of the KB, but these are formally "should not take" statements.
- **Benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, alcohol, antihistamines and antipsychotics** (all opioid SmPCs): none is in the master list. The `cns-depressant` group has no master-list members, so the cns-depressant rules never fire.
- **Paracetamol + warfarin.** The paracetamol rule stays `info`, because the paracetamol SmPCs state no action. The warfarin file (batch C) has its own `monitor` rule, so the combined panel already shows monitor. The warfarin SmPC lists paracetamol only as an example of a potentiating drug. Its "dose may need to be reduced and monitoring increased" sentence refers to metabolic inhibitors, so I did not copy that action to the paracetamol side.
- **Renal thresholds for NSAIDs and opioids**: no eGFR/CrCl number exists in these SmPCs or in SPS NSAIDs monitoring, except naproxen CrCl <30. Severe renal failure is therefore a condition rule. The engine fires it only if the user ticks "severe renal impairment", not from a typed eGFR or CrCl.
- **Duplicated cross-direction rules.** The engine evaluates rules in both directions, and batches B to D already hold most of the rules added in #13 to #19 on their own side. The rules added here are therefore partly redundant in the panel (the engine merges them by effect), but they satisfy the "both directions" requirement and they add the hyperkalaemia and warfarin-SmPC wording that the NSAID files lacked. The tramadol + ciprofloxacin rule (#10) was missing on both sides.
- **Elderly dose reduction for codeine** ("Dosage should be reduced in elderly patients") and **naproxen** ("prolonged use … in older people not recommended"): the sources give no age threshold, so no age rule was added. These remain as dosing or cautions text.

## 4. Overall assessment

- **Paracetamol:** Accurate. Doses, age bands, 4-hour interval, maximum doses and the 120 mg/5 ml vs 250 mg/5 mL age limits match the SmPCs. Completeness is good, apart from the absence of any sourced low-body-weight or hepatic-risk maximum dose. That gap is now stated honestly instead of reading as reassurance.
- **Co-codamol:** Doses and paediatric limits are accurate. The 4.3 list is complete for each SmPC after #8. The main weakness is structural: the 8/500 SmPC omits the opioid contraindications, so the 8/500 entry is less protective than the 30/500 and codeine entries.
- **Codeine:** Accurate. After #6 and #9, the contraindication and caution set matches both SmPCs. The hepatic contraindication is literal and strict (see section 2). Cough and diarrhoea doses and the 12 to 18 limits are correct.
- **Tramadol:** Accurate and fairly complete. The two main gaps were a ciprofloxacin interaction (seizures) and a St John's wort interaction (loss of effect), both now added, and the over-75 dose rule. No numeric renal threshold exists in either SmPC.
- **Ibuprofen:**
  - Doses (1200 to 2400 mg/day tablets, 1200 mg/day OTC suspension, 20 mg/kg/day, juvenile RA 30 to 40 mg/kg/day, age bands) are correct.
  - The level inconsistency for the 4.3 items is fixed.
  - Interaction coverage is now complete for master-list medicines. Before the audit it missed hyperkalaemia with spironolactone and ACE inhibitors, and renal monitoring with metformin.
- **Naproxen:**
  - Doses, CrCl <30 contraindication and paediatric limits are correct.
  - One real engine bug was fixed (#7): before the fix, any patient with normal kidney function was flagged.
  - The pregnancy wording conflict inside the Naprosyn SmPC remains visible by design.
